Deep dive

The gateway conversation: LiteLLM, Portkey, and Orca

Customers bring these names up in discovery, usually one of three ways: "we already run LiteLLM", "we evaluated Portkey", or "we are looking at gateways right now". When you do not know what those products actually are, you freeze, oversell, or back down. This hour fixes that. By the end you can explain each in plain words, concede where they genuinely win, and land the question that matters: who runs it, and what does it really cost?

The category moved twice this year: Palo Alto Networks bought Portkey (closed May 29, GA July 16 as Prisma AIRS AI Gateway). LiteLLM rewrote its core in Rust in July and only supports its four newest versions, so operators upgrade roughly monthly. "We use Portkey" can mean three different products. Always find out which one.

Three portraits, plain words

LiteLLM: the WordPress of AI gateways

Powerful, free, and everything around it is the customer's job: hosting, upgrades, security patches, monitoring. No hosted product exists to buy. The catch is never the code. It is that someone is the mechanic forever, and the moment they want audit-grade governance, the free story ends.

Portkey: the nice dashboard, now owned by the firewall company

Genuinely good observability. The meter is log volume, so the bill grows as the customer grows, and compliance starts at enterprise. Roadmap now belongs to Palo Alto. Repo has been quiet since the acquisition closed in May.

Orca: hosted, routing is the product

Change the base URL to api.orcarouter.ai/v1 and you are live. Adaptive routing that learns from real traffic, fallback chains, $0 markup, zero retention. We monetize the infrastructure, not token spend.

Make it stick

Learn

The reframe that pays for the hour: free software is not a free gateway. Someone runs it. LiteLLM charges in engineering hours and a license. Portkey charges in log volume. We charge a flat fee.

Practice (10 min, pairs)

One rep plays the LiteLLM-running platform engineer, the other runs track 1. Swap. Then the Portkey track. Goal: land the two moves, concede what is true, then ask who runs it and what it really costs.

Steal

  • "If you are on one self-hosted model with the team to run it, you don't need us."
  • "Every gateway charges you somewhere. LiteLLM charges hours, Portkey charges log volume, we charge a flat fee."
  • "We make money on the subscription, not your tokens."
Full session doc (50-min version, buyer signals, all objection tracks): The gateway conversation. Selling methodology version: Orca with the 7 Habits.
What not to say (read this one out loud in the room).
  • Do not call LiteLLM bad. It is genuinely good software, and engineers who run it will smell the lie.
  • Do not imply Portkey is dying. It is backed by Palo Alto, the opposite of dying. The honest line is ownership, not weakness.
  • Do not claim 200+ models. The verified number is 168 across 11 providers. A technical buyer will check.
  • Do not promise a savings percentage. Routing savings depend on workload mix. Never guaranteed.
  • Do not quote internal benchmarks or performance numbers in front of customers.
  • Do not bash competitors in writing. One sentence about our model beats ten about theirs in an email.
