OrcaRouter Explained

A traffic cop for AI.

When a company uses AI — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — OrcaRouter sits in between. Instead of the app talking directly to one AI, it talks to OrcaRouter, which picks the best model for every question. If one goes down, it auto-fails over. The user never notices. It's like an electrical surge protector, but for AI.

The 4 Pitches

Four ways to sell OrcaRouter.

Different audiences need different angles. Here's your playbook for each buyer persona.

#1 — Security First

Closes the most deals
If someone gets access to your company's AI bot, they get access to everything that bot has access to: passwords, client data, business information. All they need is a leaked email and password — and those are publicly available from data breaches. OrcaRouter screens every prompt, governs every tool call, and logs everything. We're the security layer between your AI and the world.
When to use: CTOs, CISOs, security-conscious companies, healthcare, finance, legal.

#2 — Cost Savings

Easiest to prove
Most companies are overpaying 30–50% on AI. They're routing every question — even 'summarize this email' — through an expensive model. OrcaRouter automatically sends simple tasks to cheap models and complex ones to powerful models. Same quality, 40% less spend. Zero code changes.
When to use: Companies with >$10K/month AI spend, cost-conscious startups, CFOs.

#3 — Reliability

Engineering-sympathetic
When OpenAI or Anthropic goes down — and they do go down — your product stops working. OrcaRouter automatically fails over to another model. Users never see a 5xx error. Your engineers stop getting paged at 2am.
When to use: Engineering-led companies, products with AI at their core, companies that have experienced outages.

#4 — Anti-Lock-In

CFO & CTO favorite
You're not locked into OpenAI or Anthropic. With OrcaRouter, you can switch models by changing one string. If a provider raises prices or degrades quality, you move. We're the escape hatch.
When to use: Procurement conversations, companies worried about vendor concentration, long-term planning discussions.

Objection Handling

What they'll say. How to answer.

Every objection has been answered in the field. Here's the playbook.

"We're happy with OpenAI. Why add a layer?"
You keep OpenAI. OrcaRouter wraps around it. Zero code changes. What you get: failover when they go down, the ability to route simple stuff to cheaper models, and enterprise security. It's insurance — and insurance that pays for itself.
"We'll build this in-house."
Respect that. Most teams spend 3–6 months building multi-model routing, streaming failover, and stream-safe guardrails. We've already solved those. Happy to share architecture patterns even if you build it yourself — that conversation alone is worth the call.
"We're too small."
The teams that need this most are the ones where one engineer owns the AI stack. When a model goes down at 2am, that engineer gets paged. OrcaRouter costs less than their time.
"What about lock-in on your side?"
It's an OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Your code speaks standard format. You can leave with zero code changes. We're the escape hatch FROM lock-in, not a new one.
"How do you make money with zero markup?"
Platform subscription. We're aligned — we succeed when your team stays and scales, not when you use more tokens. OpenRouter makes money when you spend more. We don't.
"Is this adding latency?"
Less than 1ms for routing. Less than 5ms for security checks. The AI models themselves take 500ms to 5 seconds — our overhead is invisible.
"We built model failover internally."
How are you handling degraded performance vs. full outage? What about partial failures where the model returns garbage instead of errors? OrcaRouter catches those too.
"We use OpenRouter."
OpenRouter routes. OrcaRouter governs. Zero markup either way — but only OrcaRouter ships agent firewall, PII Shield, PCI/HIPAA compliance packs, and zero data retention.
